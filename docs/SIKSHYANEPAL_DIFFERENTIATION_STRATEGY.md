# SikshyaNepal: Differentiation and Product Strategy

**Prepared:** 10 September 2026  
**Product focus:** Nepal college discovery, admissions, education decisions and verified updates  
**North star:** Become Nepal's trusted education decision infrastructure—not another directory or automated content site.

## Executive conclusion

The individual features in SikshyaNepal can be copied. The defensible system is the combination of verified field-level evidence, Nepal-specific education routes, continuously checked deadlines, real cost planning, moderated student experience, and a private action plan that follows a student from exploration to enrolment.

Large international products already demonstrate the useful patterns: BigFuture connects interests, careers, colleges and checklists; Common App turns admissions into a repeatable workflow; UCAS connects choices to deadlines; CareerOneStop publishes transparent career and training data; and review platforms aggregate student experience. SikshyaNepal should learn from those patterns without becoming a Nepal-themed copy. Its advantage must be local evidence and execution: Nepali affiliations, entrance systems, fee ambiguity, document requirements, province/district access, scholarships, and the real transition from study to work.

The best positioning is:

> **Choose a path, verify the facts, plan the real cost, and finish the application—all in one place.**

## What students are actually trying to accomplish

Students rarely want a list for its own sake. Their underlying questions are:

1. What can I study after SEE or +2, and where can it lead?
2. Am I eligible, and which entrance exam do I need?
3. Which institutions fit my location, budget and constraints?
4. Can I trust the fees, affiliation, results and deadline shown?
5. What will the full cost be after travel, housing and scholarship?
6. What documents and actions are still unfinished?
7. Will this route help me build employable skills and experience?

Nepal has a significant school-to-work information gap. World Bank reporting describes persistent skills mismatch and underemployment, while its employment programs show stronger outcomes when training connects to placement, on-the-job training and apprenticeships.[1][2] This makes career routes and opportunities core product infrastructure, not optional content.

## Competitive lessons—and SikshyaNepal's response

| Proven pattern | What it teaches | SikshyaNepal's differentiated version |
|---|---|---|
| BigFuture career and college exploration | Connect interests to saved choices and next steps | Nepal Path Graph: stage → career → course → entrance → college → opportunity |
| Common App application guidance | Students return for workflow, not static content | Admission Passport with deadlines, documents and application status |
| UCAS deadlines and choices | Dates must be attached to the exact route and intake | Verified deadline ledger with source, checked date and change history |
| CareerOneStop tools | Explain data sources and help users assess interests and skills | Transparent Nepal career pages with source-backed routes and no fake AI certainty |
| Niche-style reviews | Student voice helps only when methodology and sample size are visible | Moderated, structured reality signals with verification and response rights |
| Studyportals comparison | A large catalogue becomes useful through structured comparison | Decision check that distinguishes known facts from missing or changing facts |

## The product moat

### 1. Evidence Ledger

Every material claim should be a fact with provenance, not merely a paragraph:

- exact field and value;
- primary source URL and source owner;
- date published and date last checked;
- extraction method and confidence;
- human reviewer when relevant;
- previous value and change history;
- correction and appeal trail.

Public profiles should plainly say **Available**, **Confirm**, or **Not listed**. A missing fact is safer than a confident invention. This is particularly important for fees, affiliation, seats, eligibility, scholarship amounts, results and deadlines.

### 2. Student Decision Passport

Connect existing tools into one private student state:

- current stage and intended intake;
- preferred subjects, location and budget;
- saved routes and colleges;
- eligibility gaps;
- estimated full cost;
- documents ready or missing;
- deadlines and reminder status;
- next three actions.

Collect only what is needed. Sensitive document storage should remain out of scope until encryption, retention, access controls, incident response and legal review are mature. Initially store document checklist status, not document images.

### 3. Nepal Path Graph

Build a structured graph rather than isolated SEO pages:

`student stage → interest/goal → career → required skills → study programs → entrances → institutions → scholarships → internships`

Every node should answer a practical question and link to the next decision. Career pages must disclose uncertainty and avoid promising salaries or employment. The graph becomes difficult to copy because its value grows with verified local connections.

### 4. Real Cost and Constraint Model

Price comparison must include:

- fee amount and whether it is per semester, year or full course;
- admission, examination, laboratory and university charges;
- room/hostel, food, transport, books and devices;
- scholarship conditions and renewal rules;
- family budget gap;
- confidence level and last check for each input.

The calculator should show a range and assumptions, never a false exact total.

### 5. Moderated Reality Layer

Student experience should be structured around decisions: teaching, attendance, administration, safety, accessibility, hidden costs, transport/hostel, internship support and placement claims. Display sample size and verification type. Require moderation, allow institutional responses, and provide reporting, correction, takedown and appeal. Never generate a ranking from a tiny or unverified sample.

### 6. Freshness Operations

The ingestion system should monitor approved primary sources and identify changes every five hours, but it must not automatically publish unsupported articles. The safe pipeline is:

1. Fetch only permitted sources and retain a private evidence snapshot.
2. Extract claims and compare them with the previous version.
3. Deduplicate by canonical URL and content hash.
4. Score source authority, recency and extraction completeness.
5. Route high-impact claims to an editor.
6. Publish an original summary with citation, checked date and automation disclosure.
7. Recheck deadlines and close stale notices automatically when the primary source supports it.

Automated volume is not an SEO moat. Google explicitly warns against extensive automation used mainly to attract search visits and recommends people-first content with clear “who, how and why” disclosure.[3]

## Search, AEO and distribution

There is no special shortcut for appearing in generative search. Google's current guidance says normal SEO fundamentals and quality systems remain relevant to AI features.[4] SikshyaNepal should optimize the underlying evidence:

- one canonical page for each college, program, career, entrance and admission;
- concise answer-first summaries backed by visible sources;
- descriptive titles, headings, dates and author/reviewer identity;
- `CollegeOrUniversity`, `Course`, `Article`, `BreadcrumbList` and appropriate FAQ markup only when the content is visibly present;
- internal links that express the Path Graph;
- stable URLs and no fabricated annual “best college” lists;
- original datasets, transparent methodology and cited analysis;
- bilingual pages written natively for Nepali context rather than mechanically translated;
- editorial disclosure for AI-assisted content and a correction history.

Structured data helps search systems understand a page but does not guarantee a rich result.[5] The content must remain useful without markup.

## Experience and accessibility standard

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. The newest criteria include visible/unobscured focus, minimum target size, consistent help, reduced redundant entry and accessible authentication.[6] Product acceptance should include:

- keyboard-only completion of every form;
- persistent labels and specific errors;
- at least 44px practical touch targets on primary mobile actions;
- correct headings and landmarks;
- visible focus and sufficient contrast;
- meaningful image alternatives;
- reduced motion support;
- usable pages on narrow screens and slow connections;
- no forced sign-in for reading public information;
- sign-in only for saving, posting, reviewing, reminders or applications.

## Roadmap

### Next 30 days: trust foundation

1. Add the public Decision Check to every college profile.
2. Audit the first 50 colleges field by field; prioritize affiliation, programs, location, source and freshness.
3. Define freshness limits by field: deadlines daily, fees quarterly, affiliation semesterly, static history annually.
4. Add source/change visibility in admin and correction SLA tracking.
5. Connect compare, cost and admission checklist from every profile.
6. Measure mobile performance and accessibility on the ten highest-traffic routes.

### Days 31–90: connected journey

1. Launch Decision Passport using minimal profile data.
2. Link admissions and scholarship deadlines to saved colleges.
3. Create 20 evidence-based career paths and their Nepal study routes.
4. Upgrade cost planning with ranges, assumptions and city expenses.
5. Build moderated reality summaries with sample-size warnings.
6. Publish source-backed original explainers based on repeated student decisions—not keyword volume.

### Months 4–6: defensibility and scale

1. Expand verified college coverage and create institutional correction/claim workflows.
2. Add monitored source change detection with editorial review queues.
3. Publish anonymized, aggregate decision insights only after privacy thresholds are satisfied.
4. Add Nepali-first versions of highest-value pathways.
5. Partner with colleges, career counsellors, entrance bodies and student organizations for primary data.
6. Monetize with clearly labelled sponsored placements that never change organic trust or verification status.

## Success metrics

### Trust

- percentage of material fields with a primary source;
- percentage within freshness SLA;
- median correction resolution time;
- percentage of articles with visible source and disclosure;
- number of unsupported claims published (target: zero).

### Student outcomes

- college profile → compare/save rate;
- compare → cost-plan completion;
- saved college → admission checklist completion;
- deadline reminder return rate;
- application readiness progression;
- students who reach a verified next action.

### Quality and safety

- Core Web Vitals pass rate by template;
- WCAG 2.2 AA audit pass rate;
- moderation turnaround and upheld appeal rate;
- critical child-safety or privacy incidents (target: zero);
- stale admission notices visible after deadline.

## Explicit non-goals

SikshyaNepal should not:

- declare a “best college” without a published, defensible methodology and sufficient data;
- sell verification badges or allow sponsorship to affect factual status;
- auto-publish allegations, rankings, student results or award claims;
- use a chatbot as an authoritative career counsellor;
- scrape sources that prohibit it or republish copyrighted text;
- store unnecessary identity or sensitive student documents;
- imply that an enquiry is a completed application;
- manufacture freshness by changing dates without updating the underlying facts.

## Immediate implementation decision

The first shipped improvement is the **College Decision Check**. It does not score quality. It shows six decision-critical information states—study route, affiliation, fees, admission window, scholarships and source freshness—and gives the student three concrete next actions. This makes uncertainty visible, encourages source verification and connects the strongest existing tools into one decision flow.
